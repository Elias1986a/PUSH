How we measured

Same passage. Same Mac. Same stopwatch.

We read one passage aloud 13 times, speaking into PUSH and Wispr Flow at once, and timed how long each took to hand back the text after the key was released. These are medians of all 13 readings, not best runs. Measured 30 September 2026 on a Mac mini (M4, 24 GB), macOS 27.2, PUSH 8.2.4.

The passage

“Let's move the Q3 planning review to Tuesday, March 3rd at 4:30 PM, since that's like the only slot that works for the Zürich office. Um, we've spent 37% of the $5 million budget, and the new build loads in 15.2 seconds, I mean 1.52 seconds. Everyone who tried it said, quote, it feels like a real improvement, end quote. New paragraph. Could you like send the final numbers before Friday?”

Every phrase is there to test something: a date, a time of day, a percentage, money, decimals, an acronym, an accented place name, a filler word, a correction mid-sentence, a spoken quotation, a spoken paragraph break and a question. Two casual “like”s test PUSH's California mode, and “feels like” must survive it. Each reading took 23–29 seconds.

With its default model, PUSH is 20× faster than Wispr Flow once their network round trip is counted, and 17× faster than their server processing alone. The first number is the one on our home page. PUSH's Streaming model, which shows your words as you speak, is quicker still.

How PUSH gets there: all three models transcribe while you talk. Ultra and Unified finish each sentence as soon as you've said it; Streaming works through the audio continuously. When you let go, only the last moment is left. Decoding a whole 25-second recording from scratch instead would take Ultra 0.23 s. Both apps' numbers are the wait after the key comes up. Wispr Flow's times are its own, read from its local records; PUSH's are measured by our comparison tool on the same recordings.

Where Wispr Flow did better, we say so. Its server sometimes restores the umlaut in Zürich, and it rewrites words using the rest of the sentence: once it turned a misheard “bill” back into “build”, and once it put back a word the reader had skipped. PUSH types what it heard; the one 37% it missed came from a reading where the reader stumbled on the number. Every app, Wispr Flow included, sometimes mishears a word; the table counts only what the passage was built to test. You can switch off PUSH's self-correction in Settings ▸ Text, because it's the one step that deletes words you actually said.

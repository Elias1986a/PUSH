How we measured

Same passage. Same Mac. Same stopwatch.

We read one passage aloud three times into each app and timed how long each took to hand back the text after the key was released. These are medians of three, not best runs. Measured 29 September 2026 on [Mac model].

The passage

“Let's move the Q3 review to Tuesday, March 3rd at 4:30 PM — Larry, Priya and Joe are all in, which puts us at 37% of the $5 million target. Um, latency came back at 15.2 seconds, I mean 1.52 seconds, so ask the Zürich team to re-run it. Priya said, quote, ship it anyway, end quote. Can you confirm before Friday?”

It's built from the things dictation apps disagree about: money, times, a self-correction, a filler word, a quotation, an accented name. Each reading took 23–26 seconds.

That makes PUSH 4.6× faster than Wispr Flow's server processing alone, and 5.4× faster once their network round trip is counted. The second number is the one on our home page.

Real-time speed is seconds of speech per second of processing. Wispr Flow's figure leaves out its network time, just as ours has no network to leave out. PUSH's Streaming model measured 0.45 s, but it transcribes while you talk, so most of that is done before you let go.

Where Wispr Flow did better, we say so: its server restores the umlaut in Zürich, and PUSH misheard one phrase once in three readings. You can switch off PUSH's self-correction in Settings ▸ Text, because it's the one step that deletes words you actually said.
